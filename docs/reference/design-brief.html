Le Boat design brief

November 8, 2026 · Revision 0.3 · Concept stage

Purpose

Design and build a small RC sailboat for Love Park in Toronto. Use wind in cloth sails for all propulsion. Use a battery for the receiver and sail/rudder actuators. The DragonForce 65 provides an initial reference for the arrangement. Select dimensions from the pond, equipment, stability and fabrication limits.

The project includes CAD, home fabrication, CFD, assembly and water tests. People and AI tools develop the project together. Record tool use, design choices, calculations and test results. Contributions are welcome through repository issues and pull requests.

Tools

  • git-cad: manage CAD revisions and comparisons when the required workflow is available.
  • Monad System: supply website tokens, type, layout and UI components.
  • Hawa: model flow around the hull, keel and rudder in Rhino and Grasshopper.

Use the tool and optimization plan for version records and setup checks. git-cad is in specification/prototype development. Boat CAD management and CFD cases remain pending. Le Boat work does not change Hawa's development scope.

Use and sailing site

The operator should carry the boat and transmitter from home. Provide a removable rig, protective cradle and accessible controls. The operator must launch and recover the boat from dry land. Set cost, transport size, setup time and session endurance targets after selecting equipment.

Love Park is at 96 Queens Quay West. Waterfront Toronto's fact sheet lists pond depth as 200–600 mm, approximately 8–24 inches. The earlier 18-foot estimate conflicts with this source. Measure route depths, obstructions and fill conditions before selecting an operating area. Check operating rules with the park operator. See site research for the survey plan.

Proposed dimensions and targets

These values define the first study. Calculations and tests must determine the final requirements.

Parameter Initial study Required evidence
Hull length 350–450 mm Equipment fit, displacement, transport and stability
Loaded draft Investigate 150 mm or less, including ballast and rudder Measured route with at least 50 mm clearance. Increase margin for uncertainty and operating conditions.
Beam, hull depth and freeboard Open Hydrostatics, heel, water entry and equipment access
Displacement and ballast Open Component masses, finished structure, rig and contingency
Rig and sail area Removable rig. Consider a smaller sail option. Righting capacity, sheet travel and gust response
Wind range and endurance Open Measured wind, gusts and battery use
Cost and setup time Open BOM and timed assembly trial

At 200 mm water depth, a 150 mm draft leaves a nominal 50 mm margin. Actual clearance depends on fill level, obstructions, heel and trim. A shorter fin reduces the ballast lever arm. Recalculate stability and rig size after any fin or ballast change.

Systems and interfaces

System Design requirements
Hull and deck Displacement, trim, freeboard, sealed joints, hatch coaming, gasket, drainage and reserve flotation
Keel and ballast Lateral resistance, righting moment, ballast retention, grounding loads, secure attachment and watertight trunk
Rudder Draft, section, area, stock support, sealed penetration, actuator torque, travel and stops
Rig and sails Mast support, boom, sail patterns, rigging, tension adjustment, sail balance and sheet routing
Controls Compatible receiver, battery and actuators. Check current draw, switch access, antenna position and failure response.
Recovery and transport Visible hull, retained flotation, reachable recovery point, shore equipment, cradle and rig storage
Fabrication and maintenance Printable sections, tolerances, alignment, bonding, sealing, reinforcement, replaceable parts and repair access

The boat has no propulsion motor. Establish a recovery method for calm conditions and control failure. Keep temporary test tethers clear of people, wildlife and pond equipment. Check recovery before free sailing.

Manufacturing limits

Use a Bambu A1 mini for printed parts. The manufacturer's guide gives a nominal 180 × 180 × 180 mm build volume. Check each part, joint, support and adhesion margin in the slicer. Select shell thickness after choosing filament, nozzle, settings and reinforcement.

If the hull requires several prints, design alignment, bonding, sealing and assembly access into the joints. Include resin, laminate, coatings, adhesive, fittings and fasteners in the finished mass. Compare a printed structural shell with printed tooling before selecting the construction method.

Component choices

Component Initial option Open choices
Hull Small RC yacht arrangement Form, dimensions, waterline, segmentation and deck
Shell Printed structure with possible composite reinforcement Material, walls, orientation, joints and tooling role
Keel Fin with a symmetric airfoil section Span, chord, thickness, section, mount and load path
Ballast Weight below the hull Material, mass, shape, depth and secure retention
Rudder Stern rudder Section, area, stock, bearings, linkage and actuator
Mast Possible carbon rod Length, diameter, stiffness and support
Sails Cloth sails Material, area, pattern, rig and adjustment
Controls Radio, battery and sail/rudder actuators Components, voltage, travel, current, seals and access

Select ballast and rudder sections separately from the keel section. Check their effects on drag, stability, loads and grounding.

Composite trials

The initial proposal uses glass fabric and resin over a printed structure, with vacuum-bag consolidation. The original material note says “75oz fiberglass.” Check the intended fabric weight and units before buying material or estimating laminate mass. This specification remains unresolved.

Select reinforced areas, resin, layer count, fibre directions and cure conditions through sample tests. Support prints during bagging and check their resistance to vacuum pressure. Record finished sample mass, deformation, bonding and water resistance. Select the construction method after these trials.

Engineering and optimization

Create a complete component mass budget before selecting ballast. Calculate displacement, trim, centres of mass and buoyancy, freeboard and stability. Check mast, keel, rudder and joint loads. Check water entry during heel and recovery after knockdown.

Use Hawa to compare flow around the hull and appendages. Start with an explicitly submerged case and simple appendage studies. Compare candidates at fixed physical conditions. Check grid sensitivity, domain sensitivity and convergence. Record pressure, velocity, drag and lateral force where supported.

Optimize drag and lateral resistance within limits for draft, stability, mass and fabrication. Compare print material, print time, joint count, tacking and steering response. Record trade-offs and uncertainty. Use physical tests to check selected designs.

A submerged single-fluid case omits free-surface waves and sail-driven motion. These effects need suitable models or experiments. Use separate structural, hydrostatic and fabrication checks. See the optimization procedure.

Test requirements

Follow the build and test plan. Set acceptance criteria before each test. Record the configuration, conditions, measurements and failures.

The first sailing prototype must meet the selected flotation, trim, stability and leak criteria. It must respond to rudder and sail controls. It must sail in both directions, tack and permit recovery from dry land. Boat tests remain pending. Wind and endurance limits remain open.

Documentation and collaboration

Keep editable CAD, scripts, exports, drawings, BOM, print settings, sail patterns and assembly instructions. Use git-cad revision records when the required workflow is available. Link each simulation and test to its geometry and settings. Publish design changes and build results in the GitHub Pages journal. Label planned work, renders, simulations and physical results.

Use short, direct English with ASD-STE100 sentence structure. Keep AI use visible in the project record. Record tools, significant contributions, completed checks and uncertainty. Invite contributions in CAD, CFD, fabrication, sails, electronics and testing. See CONTRIBUTING.md.
