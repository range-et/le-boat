Commercial permission

The project owner's policy is that commercial reuse outside the published grants requires separate written permission. Attribution by itself is insufficient. Describe the intended use, files, modifications, distribution and scale in a repository issue. Permission must come from all relevant rights holders; the maintainer cannot license someone else's rights. No separate commercial grant is made here.

Read the actual licenses for their definitions and exceptions. CC NonCommercial concerns commercial advantage or monetary compensation, not merely whether an activity makes a profit. PolyForm also expressly permits certain organization uses; its definitions and permissions differ from CC. This policy summary does not add restrictions to either standard license. Noncommercial restrictions mean the project should be described as source-available, not OSI open source or unrestricted open hardware.

Limits and choice of licenses

CC is used for design/documentation; a software-specific license is used for code, consistent with Creative Commons' software guidance. MIT, Apache and CERN OHL permit commercial reuse and therefore would not reserve commercial permission as requested. ShareAlike keeps shared CC adaptations under matching terms; it does not make the code copyleft.

These licenses operate only where the relevant legal rights exist. Functional boat features, manufactured objects and AI-assisted output can raise copyright, design-right and other questions. CC does not grant patent or trademark rights. Publishing under an NC license cannot guarantee control over every physical boat or every use of an idea. Obtain legal review before relying on this arrangement for commercial manufacture or enforcement.

Both licenses include warranty/liability language; see also DISCLAIMER.md. Such language applies only as far as the law allows, and cannot guarantee that no liability can arise. Standard license grants generally cannot simply be withdrawn from recipients already complying with them. Contributors retain their rights; submit only material you can license.
