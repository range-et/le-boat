# Love Park site research

Research date: November 8, 2026.
Published descriptions provide background information.
An on-site survey and operating checks remain pending.

## Location and depth

[Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Love+Park+Toronto) identifies Love Park at 96 Queens Quay West.
The [City park listing](https://www.toronto.ca/explore-enjoy/parks-recreation/places-spaces/parks-and-recreation-facilities/location/?id=3799) places it at York Street and Queens Quay West.

[Waterfront Toronto's fact sheet](https://www.waterfrontoronto.ca/sites/default/files/2023-06/Love%20Park%20Fact%20Sheet_0.pdf) gives pond depth as **20–60 cm**, approximately **8–24 inches**.
The earlier description of an 18-foot deep end conflicts with this source.
Use the published range as background until route measurements are available.

The [Waterfront Toronto project page](https://www.waterfrontoronto.ca/our-projects/love-park) describes a heart-shaped pond and central island.
Check turning space and recovery access around these features.
Map images do not establish water depth or clearance.

Account for the lowest keel, ballast and rudder points.
Include heel, trim, debris and water-level variation when selecting clearance.
Published depth does not guarantee current fill level or an unobstructed route.

## Operating rules

[Toronto Municipal Code Chapter 608, section 608-19](https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/municode/1184_608.pdf) addresses powered models and permit requirements in parks.
The project needs the operator's interpretation for a wind-propelled model with radio servos.
Check this question before sailing.
Check posted rules, closures and launch arrangements.
The project team did not contact the operator during this research.

## Site visit procedure

1. Record the date, fill condition and posted restrictions.
2. Ask the operator about model sailing, radio controls, seasonal filling and maintenance.
3. Identify a launch point and recovery positions on dry land.
4. Obtain permission for measurements where required.
5. Measure depth along a short route within recovery reach.
6. Mark obstructions, intakes, outlets and excluded areas on a sketch.
7. Record the measurement method and uncertainty.
8. Observe wind changes and calm areas around trees and buildings.

Keep public paths clear.
Avoid routes that require wading, climbing onto the pond edge or entering planting.
Stop trials around wildlife or crowded edges.
A single visit provides limited evidence of wind conditions.

The survey record must show measured depths, the usable route, excluded areas and recovery positions.
Attach the operating-rule response.
The proposed 150 mm draft remains provisional until these checks are complete.
